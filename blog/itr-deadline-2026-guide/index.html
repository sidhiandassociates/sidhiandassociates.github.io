⏰ ITR Filing Deadline 2026 - What Happens If You Miss It

📅 Updated: August 4, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ By CA Experts

For FY 2025-26: salaried taxpayers (ITR-1/ITR-2) had until 31 July 2026. Business/professional taxpayers without audit (ITR-3/ITR-4) have until 31 August 2026 — extended under Budget 2026. Audit cases: 31 October. Missed your deadline? A belated return is still possible until 31 December 2026, with a late fee.

The general ITR deadline for salaried individuals (ITR-1/ITR-2) for FY 2025-26 was 31 July 2026, and it was not extended. However, taxpayers with business or professional income filing ITR-3 or ITR-4 without an audit requirement got an extra month — their deadline was pushed to 31 August 2026 as part of Budget 2026 amendments. If you've already missed your applicable deadline, you're not out of options: a belated return is still possible until 31 December 2026, just with a late fee and interest attached.

💡 If you're filing ITR-3 or ITR-4 without an audit requirement, you still have until 31 August 2026 — don't wait for the last day given portal congestion in past years.

1️⃣ Every Deadline for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27)

Taxpayer Category Due Date
Salaried individuals, pensioners (ITR-1, ITR-2)31 July 2026 (passed)
Business/professional income, no audit required (ITR-3, ITR-4)31 August 2026
Taxpayers requiring statutory audit31 October 2026
Transfer pricing cases (international/specified domestic transactions)30 November 2026
📌 Why the August 31 extension only applies to some taxpayers: The extension for ITR-3/ITR-4 non-audit filers was announced as part of Budget 2026 amendments, specifically to give business owners and professionals more time to finalize accounts, reconcile transactions, and prepare financial statements — a process salaried individuals with simpler income don't need. If you have multiple income sources, verify which category actually applies to you before assuming you have extra time.

2️⃣ Already Missed Your Deadline? Here's What Happens

Late Fee (Section 234F)

Up to ₹5,000 if your total income exceeds ₹5 lakh. Reduced to ₹1,000 if your total income is ₹5 lakh or below.

Interest (Section 234A)

1% per month (or part of a month) on any unpaid tax amount, calculated from the original due date until you actually file.

⚠️ Other Real Costs of Filing Late

Beyond the direct penalty, late filing can cost you the ability to carry forward certain losses (business losses, capital losses) to offset against future income — house property loss is typically an exception and can still be carried forward. Your refund processing (if you're owed one) also gets delayed, since it only begins after your return is filed and processed.

3️⃣ Your Options If You've Already Missed the Deadline

📝

Belated Return

File under Section 139(4) until 31 December 2026. Late fee + interest apply, but you're still within the normal filing window.

✏️

Revised Return

Found an error after filing (belated or on-time)? A revised return can generally be filed until 31 March 2027.

🔄

Updated Return (ITR-U)

Missed even the belated deadline? File within 4 years from the end of the assessment year — but with additional tax of 25-70%, and it can't be used to claim a refund.

⚠️ ITR-U is a last resort, not a strategy: The additional tax under ITR-U scales up the longer you wait — roughly 25% if filed within the first year after the belated deadline, rising to 70% if filed in the final year of the 4-year window. It also cannot be used to reduce your tax liability or claim a refund, only to voluntarily disclose additional income. File on time, or at least file a belated return, rather than defaulting to ITR-U.

4️⃣ Don't Forget E-Verification

Filing your ITR isn't complete until it's e-verified — and this must happen within 30 days of filing for the return to be considered validly filed on that date. If you e-verify late, your filing date shifts to the verification date, which can itself trigger late fees if it pushes you past your deadline. Verify immediately after filing via Aadhaar OTP, net banking, or the other methods available on the e-filing portal.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: I'm salaried and missed July 31 - what do I do?

Answer: File a belated return under Section 139(4) as soon as possible, and by 31 December 2026 at the latest. You'll pay a late fee (₹1,000-₹5,000 depending on income) plus 1% monthly interest on any unpaid tax, but you're still well within the normal filing window.

Q2: Do I get the August 31 extension if I'm salaried but also have some freelance income?

Answer: It depends on which ITR form actually applies to you and whether your accounts require an audit. This is exactly the kind of borderline case worth checking with a CA before you assume you have extra time - filing under the wrong assumption can itself trigger late fees.

Q3: Will refunds be delayed if I file a belated return?

Answer: Yes, typically - refund processing only starts once your return is filed and processed, so a later filing date generally means a later refund, on top of the late fee and interest you'll owe if there's unpaid tax.

Q4: Is there any way to avoid the late fee entirely?

Answer: Not once the applicable deadline has passed - Section 234F applies automatically based on your filing date. The only way to avoid it is filing on time in the first place, which is why we recommend not waiting until the last day even with the extended August 31 deadline.
